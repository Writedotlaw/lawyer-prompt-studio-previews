# Use the conversation to develop better work.
> A useful AI exchange often continues after the first answer. Give the system something specific to work on, compare what it returns, and use your judgment to direct the next round.

## Choose the practices that help with the assignment.

Think about improving the opening of a client recommendation. You could ask for a rewrite and accept the version that sounds best. Or you could identify what the opening needs to accomplish, give the tool an example of the approach you have in mind, and ask for alternatives that emphasize different points. The second approach gives you more to evaluate and a clearer basis for choosing.

The six practices below describe that way of working. Begin with enough of your own work to direct the task. Give guidance and explain useful examples. Compare options and follow up on what needs to change. Ask for additions, objections, or overlooked problems. Keep each request manageable. Then combine those choices in instructions suited to the assignment.

You do not need six messages every time. A sentence edit may need only your diagnosis, an example, and a follow-up. A difficult analysis may need several rounds in which you return to the sources and revise your own understanding. Select a practice below to see how it works and read a request you could adapt.

## Ask for alternatives that expose a writing decision.

Five versions of the same sentence may differ only in their verbs. Sometimes that is useful. But for a heading or opening paragraph, ask for alternatives that help you examine the point you are making.

Suppose the recommendation depends on an unresolved fact. One opening could lead with the inability to confirm the answer. Another could begin with the information the client needs to provide. A third might explain the practical risk of proceeding before that information arrives. All can preserve the same substance while directing the reader's attention differently.

Ask the tool to explain those differences, then examine the explanations yourself. Which version helps this reader make the decision? Which one might imply greater certainty than the evidence supports? After choosing, tell the system what to preserve. Otherwise, the next rewrite may reopen a choice you have already made.

## Make the follow-up as specific as the problem.

When a revision almost works, identify where it succeeds before explaining what remains wrong. This helps you preserve a useful change instead of restarting the whole passage.

```prompt
The opening now makes the recommendation easy to find, but it treats the notice date as settled. Keep the opening's organization. Explain that the record establishes sending, not receipt, and connect that gap to the next step. Do not invent a receipt date or imply that the other termination requirements are satisfied.
```

Compare that with “try again, but be more careful.” The specific instruction tells the tool which fact needs attention, what meaning must survive, and which part of the draft you have accepted. It also gives you a focused question for reviewing the next response: did it preserve the sending–receipt distinction without undoing the useful opening?

A follow-up can question the analysis, too. Ask why a proposed distinction matters or which source supports an inference. The conversation should help you investigate the work, not merely cycle through more polished versions.

## Keep each round small enough to review.

A request to rewrite ten pages can change the argument, organization, and wording at once. Even a capable response leaves you with a demanding comparison. You need to recognize which changes improve the document and which alter something that should remain.

Choose a unit that lets you make those decisions. You might settle the order of the issues before drafting paragraphs, or work on the headings before revising the analysis beneath them. For a dense provision, first identify the condition and exception, then ask how to explain their relationship to the client.

Smaller is not automatically better. An exception should not be separated from the rule it qualifies, and related agreements may need to be read together. The right size is the amount of work you can meaningfully direct and check. Increase it when your testing and experience give you a sound reason to do so.

## Use the frameworks for the different questions they answer.

The guide uses several frameworks, and they have different jobs. The **five-stage responsible-use process** covers an assignment from beginning to end. First, form your own initial understanding. Use AI to develop or challenge that work. Review the substance against appropriate evidence. Make the decision and revise the result yourself. Then record what you learned when the work warrants a record.

The **six collaboration practices** in this chapter help you conduct the exchanges within that larger process. They address choices such as when to ask for alternatives and how to give feedback. The **five-question before-use check** helps you decide whether a particular use is appropriate in the first place.

Later, the **agent work order** gives you a place to specify a multi-step assignment, including permissions and required approvals. It is more detailed because a system acting across several steps needs clearer limits than a single request for a sentence revision.

Use the framework that answers the question in front of you. You are learning how to make these decisions, not accumulating forms to complete before every prompt.
